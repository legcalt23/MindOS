# Scientific Validity Review

This document evaluates the scientific plausibility, cognitive mechanisms, limitations, and validation pathways for each of the proposed systems in the MindBoost Cognitive Disruption Framework.

---

## 1. System Evaluation & Classification

### A. Adversarial Heuristic Saboteur (AHS)
*   **Classification:** **PLAUSIBLE HYPOTHESIS**
*   **Evidence Strength:** **Moderate** (Academic research in task-switching, cognitive flexibility, and cognitive control under paradigm shifts support that randomizing stimulus-response mappings prevents procedural automation).
*   **Likitive Cognitive Mechanism:** Re-engagement of the Dorsolateral Prefrontal Cortex (dlPFC) and Anterior Cingulate Cortex (ACC) by disrupting automated motor heuristics executed by the Basal Ganglia.
*   **Pseudoscience Risk:** Low, as long as it is framed as "preventing game-specific practice effects" rather than "boosting general brain capacity."
*   **Measurement Difficulty:** Low. Tracked by response time spikes during rule switches.
*   **Transfer Plausibility:** High. Directly targets cognitive flexibility and set-shifting, which are vital for adapting to sudden environment changes.
*   **User Harm/Fatigue Risk:** High user frustration. Can lead to early abandonment if not introduced with appropriate narrative justification.
*   **Minimum Viable Version:** A visual stimulus-matching game where key-bindings or target colors swap dynamically every $N$ correct trials without warning.
*   **Required Evidence for Claims:** Internal study showing that users trained with AHS maintain a higher response time variance but exhibit lower "learning curve plateaus" compared to standard training.

### B. Sensor-less Cognitive Load Estimator (SCLE)
*   **Classification:** **SPECULATIVE / HIGH RESEARCH NEED**
*   **Evidence Strength:** **Low-Moderate** (Keystroke dynamics and touch pressure analysis exist in cybersecurity for identity validation, and reaction speed variance is linked to fatigue, but mapping touch dynamics *directly* to cognitive fatigue in real-time is speculative).
*   **Likitive Cognitive Mechanism:** Attentional lapses (micro-lapses) manifesting as high standard deviation in reaction times (Coefficient of Variation) and motor jitter (micro-tremors) due to motor cortex exhaustion under low dopamine/sleep states.
*   **Pseudoscience Risk:** Moderate-High. Claiming to know exact cognitive load without EEG/pupillometry risks being labeled as pseudoscience if overstated.
*   **Measurement Difficulty:** Extremely high. Motor inputs are highly noisy, and physical device factors (touch screen sensitivity, finger sweat) interfere.
*   **Transfer Plausibility:** N/A (Diagnostic tool, not training tool).
*   **User Harm/Fatigue Risk:** Low (Passive tracking).
*   **Minimum Viable Version:** Focus solely on local reaction time consistency (standard deviation of reaction times during E6 blocks) rather than touch pressure or typing jitter.
*   **Required Evidence for Claims:** Correlating standard deviation of reaction times with Sleep Deprivation Scales (PVT - Psychomotor Vigilance Task) before presenting it to users.

### C. Probabilistic Intuition Engine (PIE)
*   **Classification:** **PROVEN FACT (Decision-making drill)**
*   **Evidence Strength:** **High** (Bayesian updating tasks, Iowa Gambling Task, and decision-making under risk are standard neuropsychological paradigms).
*   **Likitive Cognitive Mechanism:** Ventromedial Prefrontal Cortex (vmPFC) and Anterior Insula activation, governing risk valuation, probability integration, and feedback processing.
*   **Pseudoscience Risk:** Low. Based on established probability theory.
*   **Measurement Difficulty:** Moderate. Score is determined by information gain efficiency (comparing user decisions to the optimal mathematical Bayesian path).
*   **Transfer Plausibility:** High. Direct transfer to logical risk-assessment tasks, programming debugging, and trading.
*   **User Harm/Fatigue Risk:** Low. Highly engaging for analytical minds.
*   **Minimum Viable Version:** A simplified 2-choice card/box draw task where the odds of a win dynamically mutate, and the user must bet/guess while expressing confidence.
*   **Required Evidence for Claims:** None needed for generic training claims; needed only if claiming it improves trading profitability.

### D. Adrenergic Attentional Overload (AAO) & Crisis Mode
*   **Classification:** **STRATEGIC BET**
*   **Evidence Strength:** **Moderate** (Yerkes-Dodson Law dictates the relationship between arousal and performance. Attentional narrowing under high stress is highly documented).
*   **Likitive Cognitive Mechanism:** Executive control under sympathetic nervous system activation, requiring the dlPFC to inhibit Amygdala-driven distraction and panic responses.
*   **Pseudoscience Risk:** Low, if framed as stress-resilience drills.
*   **Measurement Difficulty:** Moderate. Calculated by comparing accuracy drop-off and reaction time changes between the standard mode and the overloaded mode.
*   **Transfer Plausibility:** High for high-pressure professionals (military, emergency medicine, high-frequency traders).
*   **User Harm/Fatigue Risk:** Extremely high. Can trigger real anxiety and performance panic.
*   **Minimum Viable Version:** A 3-minute block of E6 (Multivía) where target speed increases, flashing red/white visual borders appear on errors, and high-frequency alert tones play.
*   **Required Evidence for Claims:** Comparative testing showing that users trained under stress maintain higher accuracy in subsequent high-pressure tests compared to control groups.

### E. Metacognitive Gating Coordinator (MGC)
*   **Classification:** **PLAUSIBLE HYPOTHESIS**
*   **Evidence Strength:** **Moderate** (Self-monitoring and task allocation rely on rostrolateral prefrontal cortex activity, but structured software metrics for it are early-stage).
*   **Likitive Cognitive Mechanism:** Rostrolateral Prefrontal Cortex (rlPFC / Brodmann Area 10) recruitment for hierarchical goal-management and energy allocation.
*   **Pseudoscience Risk:** Low, unless claiming to cure executive dysfunction.
*   **Measurement Difficulty:** High.
*   **Transfer Plausibility:** High for multi-tasking management.
*   **User Harm/Fatigue Risk:** Moderate.
*   **Minimum Viable Version:** Delay. Too complex for MVP.

## 2. Weak & Prohibited Claims

### Prohibited Claims (No-Go):
*   *“MindBoost increases fluid intelligence (IQ) by X%.”* (Unproven, scientifically controversial, and a target for FTC regulatory actions).
*   *“MindBoost diagnoses or treats ADHD, burnout, or cognitive decline.”* (Classifies the app as a Medical Device, triggering strict FDA/regulatory review).
*   *“Improve memory capacity forever in 7 days.”* (False, unsustainable).

### Allowed Claims (Safe):
*   *“Measure daily cognitive baseline variance to determine daily readiness.”* (Strictly diagnostic and deterministic).
*   *“Train attention control and response consistency under simulated stress.”* (Procedural, skill-based).
*   *“Block muscle-memory shortcuts during training to maintain cognitive engagement.”* (Relates strictly to the AHS mechanic).
